This is Quay Quarter Tower, formerly the AMP Centre, built in 1976. It was originally destined for obsolescence, but the developer launched a program called "upcycling," turning it into the world's first comprehensively upgraded skyscraper. The core principle of the retrofit can be summed up in eight words: maximum retention, high-quality upgrading. In the end, 65% of the main structure and 95% of the core were retained. The design firm 3XN puts it plainly: the skyscrapers built in the latter half of the 20th century are gradually reaching the end of their design lives. We must extend their lifespans and unlock their latent value—that is the most fundamental definition of architectural sustainability.
