The most classic cautionary tale is the Pruitt-Igoe housing complex in the United States. This model community consisted of 33 high-rise buildings and even received architectural awards when it was completed. But as public spaces went neglected and facilities fell into disrepair, affluent residents gradually moved out. The community slowly became home to lower-income residents and newcomers, while crime rates continued to rise. In 1972, all 33 high-rises were demolished by controlled explosions. From model community to demolition took just 18 years.
