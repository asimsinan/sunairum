Pull your gaze back a little, and you’ll see that this is really a form of structural aging. High-rise residential buildings that once symbolized the prosperity of urbanization begin to show concentrated risks in their structure, fire safety, elevators, and exterior walls after a decade or more. The burden of maintenance also grows increasingly heavy. One clear signal is that fewer people are willing to live in high-rises. The idea that “high-rise housing is the slums of the future” has already become a real cautionary lesson in some developed Western countries.
