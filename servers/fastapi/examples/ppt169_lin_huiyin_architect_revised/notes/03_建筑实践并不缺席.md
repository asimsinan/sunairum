[Transition] Many people say that Lin Huiyin had "few works," and consequently question whether she deserves to be called an architect.

The problem with this judgment is that it looks only at the number of completed works, not at the conditions of practice. Liang Qichao's tombstone in 1929 marked her starting point after returning to China; projects from the same year, such as Shitou Lou and the Jihai Railway Station, show that she had already entered the practice of modern public and campus architecture. Later, from Peking University to Yingqiu Courtyard and then to her wartime home in Kunming, her architectural work never truly stopped.

[Pause]

So the question is not whether she participated in practice, but how war, education, research, and the division of labor in reality constrained the many opportunities she had to see her work completed.
