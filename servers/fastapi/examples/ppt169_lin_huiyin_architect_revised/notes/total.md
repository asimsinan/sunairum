# 01_Cover

Today, we revisit Lin Huiyin—not through legend or gossip, but by starting with a more accurate identity: architect.

[Pause]

Seventy years after her death, she is still often surrounded by the halo of literature, emotional narratives, and the imagined figure of the “talented woman of Republican-era China.” But if we place her back in the history of modern Chinese architecture, her position is actually quite clear: she not only participated in architectural practice, but also in theoretical development and the founding of the discipline.

This short talk isn't meant to “add another chapter to the story,” but to correct a title.

Key points: ① The theme is not legend, but correcting identity ② Clearly establish three main lines: work, theory, and action ③ Set a professional tone for the entire presentation

# 02_Why We Need to Re-examine Lin Huiyin Today

[Transition] Before turning to specific works, let’s answer one question: Why do we still need to rediscover Lin Huiyin today?

Because for so long, what was repeatedly told was not always the most important part of her story. In the public memory, she is often first remembered as a gifted woman, a poet, or a figure in a love story. But what truly underpins her place in history is her architectural practice, theoretical work, and educational institution-building.

[Pause]

So this is not merely an “addendum of biographical anecdotes,” but a correction to the coordinates of knowledge.

Key points: ① Distinguish public image from professional standing ② Clarify that “reframing” is not an emotional gesture, but a correction of knowledge ③ Establish a framework for evaluating the content that follows
Duration: 1 minute

# 03_Architectural Practice Was by No Means Absent

[Transition] Many people say that Lin Huiyin had "few works," and consequently question whether she deserves to be called an architect.

The problem with this judgment is that it looks only at the number of completed works, not at the conditions of practice. Liang Qichao's tombstone in 1929 marked her starting point after returning to China; projects from the same year, such as Shitou Lou and the Jihai Railway Station, show that she had already entered the practice of modern public and campus architecture. Later, from Peking University to Yingqiu Courtyard and then to her wartime home in Kunming, her architectural work never truly stopped.

[Pause]

So the question is not whether she participated in practice, but how war, education, research, and the division of labor in reality constrained the many opportunities she had to see her work completed.

Key points: ① Respond to doubts about having “few works” ② Establish a sustained trajectory of practice ③ Reframe the question of quantity as one of historical circumstances
Duration: 1.5 minutes

# 04_Project in Context: Architecture Is Not an Abstract Noun

[Transition] If we return our focus to specific sites, we can see more clearly how she worked.

Jihai Railway Terminal treats architecture as a public symbol; the Peking University Geology Museum reflects modernism and an awareness of the user's scale; Yingqiu Courtyard responds to reality through local materials and courtyard organization; the Kunming Residence is an example of maintaining an architectural order under extreme conditions.

[Pause]

These projects show that Lin Huiyin was not an abstract “figure in architectural history,” but an architect who consistently dealt with real spatial problems.

Key points: ① Replace abstract evaluation with four real-world sites ② Emphasize that she faced different types of challenges ③ Bring the identity of “architect” from concept back to the site
Duration: 1.5 minutes

# 05_Architectural Engagement at the National Scale

[Transition] Lin Huiyin's architectural work later expanded to a broader national scale.

The design of the principal layout of Babaoshan Revolutionary Cemetery, along with the key revisions and pattern treatments for the Monument to the People's Heroes, shows that she did not play a marginal role in national memorial architecture. In particular, her adjustments to the monument's base, steps, and scale relationships demonstrate a clear command of spatial judgment.

[Pause]

This also means that Lin Huiyin's architectural work was not limited to academic research; she directly participated in constructing spaces of national memory.

Key points: ① Highlight her substantive contributions to national memorial architecture ② Emphasize judgments of scale and order ③ Elevate her from a “participant” to a “key decision-maker”
Duration: 1.2 minutes

---

# 06_She Was Not an Assistant, but a Theorist in Her Own Right

[Transition] But if we look only at her architectural projects, we still underestimate Lin Huiyin.

In 1932, she published “Several Characteristics of Chinese Architecture.” This was not an article introducing Chinese architecture, but the first systematic definition of Chinese architecture within the discourse of Chinese scholars. She challenged Western misinterpretations, identified the key features of timber construction, and laid the theoretical foundation for later narratives of Chinese architectural history.

[Pause]

So, more accurately, she was not supplementing someone else’s theory from behind, but helping shape the theory itself.

Key points: ① Explain the paper’s historical significance ② Highlight the two actions of “defining” and “refuting” ③ Correct the perception of her as merely an “assistant”
Duration: 1.5 minutes

---

# 07_Disciplines, Methods, and Foresight

[Transition] What Lin Huiyin truly left behind may not have been a single iconic work, but an entire methodology.

She brought together surveying, fieldwork, historical materials, and typological analysis; helped establish architecture departments and curricula, institutionalizing architectural education; and focused on vernacular preservation and ordinary people’s housing, proposing concepts such as “architectural meaning.” Many propositions that seem self-evident today required extraordinary foresight at the time.

[Pause]

Precisely because of this, her significance cannot be measured solely by the number of works she produced; it must also be judged by how she changed the way this discipline is studied, taught, and understood.

Key points: ① Three dimensions: methodology, discipline, and vision ② Emphasis on its institutional and long-term impact ③ A deeper assessment of her historical position
Duration: 1.5 minutes

---

# 08_How She Survived War and Illness

[Transition] Looking beyond personality, what matters most about Lin Huiyin is not her “legendary aura,” but her ability to take action.

She crossed mountains and rivers to conduct fieldwork—not as a companion, but as a collaborator; despite illness and displacement, she never stopped writing, researching, or teaching. Behind her words, “I still have so much to do,” was a clear sense of purpose.

[Pause]

So her “strength” was not a matter of posture, but of professional ethics. She pressed her life into the historical moment when Chinese architecture was taking shape.

Key points: ① Break the image of the “passive woman” ② Highlight action and professional mission ③ Provide a personal foundation for all the professional judgments made earlier
Duration: 1.2 minutes

# 09_Conclusion

[Transition] Finally, let’s return to that gravestone.

“Architect Lin Huiyin’s tomb.” These seven words are already precise enough. They remove her from an overly sentimental narrative and place her back in a more solid position: architect, proposer of theories, and builder of the discipline.

[Pause]

To understand her anew is not to create a new myth, but to describe, in more precise language, someone who was important all along.

Key points: ① Return to the wording on the tombstone to bring the presentation to a close ② Reaffirm the subject’s professional identity ③ End the presentation with “accurate naming”
