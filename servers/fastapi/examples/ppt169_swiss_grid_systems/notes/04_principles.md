So what are the core principles of this style? Müller-Brockmann summarized them in eight points. First, objectivity comes before expression — conveying information takes priority over the designer’s personal emotions. Second, the mathematical grid serves as the framework — columns, rows, spacing, and modules are all derived from basic units. Third, asymmetrical composition — rejecting the central authority implied by classical symmetry. Fourth, sans-serif typefaces. Fifth, flush left and ragged right, with even letter spacing. Sixth, objective photography replaces decorative illustration. Seventh, whitespace is structure, not leftovers — this is the most overlooked and hardest-to-achieve principle in the entire system, so we will devote a separate slide to it later. Eighth, it should be repeatable, teachable, and scalable.
