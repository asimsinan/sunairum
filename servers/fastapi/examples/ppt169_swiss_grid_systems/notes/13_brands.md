Look at the products you use every day, and you’ll find Swiss DNA everywhere. Apple is the most visible heir: from the Keynote presentations of the Jobs era to today’s Human Interface Guidelines, minimalist rationality forms the foundation of its product language. Google’s Material Design system brings Swiss engineering to mobile through an 8dp grid and geometric type. Spotify uses oversized type and grid-based blocks to turn music into a visual story—a continuation of the magazine aesthetic. Airbnb’s DLS design language system uses a modular grid and minimalist identity; even its photography follows an objective, documentary approach. IBM is direct lineage—Müller-Brockmann himself served as a consultant there in 1967, while Paul Rand took over its identity work around the same time. Lufthansa’s visual identity was created by Otl Aicher in 1962 and has remained largely intact for more than six decades. Half a century later, this grammar is still the default coordinate system for contemporary brands.
