Müller-Brockmann categorized grids into four basic types. The first is the manuscript grid: a single full-width column, the oldest type, and virtually the only option for book text and long-form prose. The second is the column grid: multiple columns arranged side by side, with column widths, line heights, and spacing all calculable—the grammatical foundation of periodical and newspaper publishing. The third is the modular grid—a matrix of squares formed by horizontal rows and vertical columns, a powerful tool for posters and catalogs and the most recognizable grid type of Swiss Internationalism. The small red square in the lower-right corner marks the logic of the cover image used as the background for today's lecture. The fourth is the hierarchical grid: irregular but internally logical, used for composite layouts, magazine features, and web design. The common premise of all four grids can be summed up in one sentence: every distance is calculable.
