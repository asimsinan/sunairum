Let’s take a closer look at Müller-Brockmann himself. Born in 1914 in the small Swiss town of Rapperswil, about 40 kilometers from Zurich, he opened an independent studio in Zurich in 1936 at the age of 22, working across three fields: graphic design, exhibitions, and photography. The real turning point came in 1950, when he began designing the Musica Viva poster series for Zurich’s Tonhalle, using pure geometric elements—circles, arcs, lines, and rectangles—to express musical structures in abstract form. To this day, the series remains a textbook example of constructivist poster design. In 1957, he became a professor at the Zurich School of Applied Arts. In 1958, together with three like-minded designers, he founded the trilingual journal Neue Grafik, formally bringing Swiss ideas to the international design community. In 1981, he published Grid Systems in Graphic Design—the foundational text for today’s lecture. He died in Unterengstringen in 1996 at the age of 82.
