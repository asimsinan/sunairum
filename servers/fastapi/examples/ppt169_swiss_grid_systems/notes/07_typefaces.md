After the people, let’s look at three typefaces. Akzidenz-Grotesk was released in 1896 by the Berthold type foundry in Berlin—the first commercial sans-serif typeface in the modern sense. It predates Helvetica by a full 61 years and established the fundamental tone of sans-serif aesthetics. Helvetica was designed by Miedinger and Hoffmann in 1957. Its name literally means “Swiss,” from the Latin Helvetia. Neutral, objective, and exceptionally legible, it became the most widely disseminated typeface of the twentieth century. Also in 1957, Adrian Frutiger created Univers. For the first time, he systematized “family, weight, and width” through a two-dimensional numbering system with 21 weights, making it the ancestor of the modern type family structure. Within a single year, Switzerland produced two decisive typefaces—an eruption of an era.
