Many people think the Swiss International Typographic Style emerged overnight, created by a handful of master designers. In fact, it was the result of six full decades of accumulation. In 1896, Berlin’s Berthold Type Foundry released Akzidenz-Grotesk, the first commercial sans-serif typeface in the modern sense. In 1908, the Basel School of Design completed its reforms. In 1918, Ernst Keller joined the Zurich School of Arts and Crafts, establishing the teaching principle of "form follows content." In 1936, 22-year-old Müller-Brockmann opened an independent studio in Zurich. Finally, in 1957, Helvetica and Univers were released in the same year. Rationalism did not appear out of nowhere; it was the fruit that grew over half a century from three intertwined threads: typefaces, schools, and teaching.
