[Transition] The first paper was only the beginning; Lin Huiyin's academic vision extended far beyond that of her contemporaries.

She pioneered the concept of “architectural meaning,” arguing that architecture was not merely a combination of technology and beauty, but also a crystallization of history and human feeling. She wrote the introduction to Qing Structural Regulations, systematically outlining the framework of Chinese architectural theory. She was the first in China’s architectural field to advocate the preservation of vernacular buildings. In 1945, she published “Reference for Modern Residential Design,” presciently proposing that postwar architecture should be designed for ordinary people.

[Pause]

In 1949, she launched Tsinghua University’s first “Introduction to Housing” course, systematically teaching modern residential design—ideas that seem self-evident today required extraordinary foresight more than 70 years ago.
