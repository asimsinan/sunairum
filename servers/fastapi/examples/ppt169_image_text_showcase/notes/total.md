# 01_Cover

The theme of this presentation booklet is the combination of text and image—we use twenty pages to showcase twenty completely different visual languages. Each page is an independent experiment, with a different rendering style, color palette, and approach to arranging text and images.

---

# 02_Contents

From the classic image-left, text-right layout to Z-shaped serpentine storytelling, from circular radial callouts to diagonal splits, and finally to a single-line blackboard ending, you’ll see how many different ways images and text can be combined.

---

# 03_Image Left, Text Right

The most traditional text-and-image layout—an upright portrait on the left, with the main body of text unfolding on the right. The realism of editorial photography and its cool, professional tones create the feel of a business presentation. The image-to-text ratio is approximately three to seven: the image is understated while the text carries the weight, making it suitable for content-led pages.

---

# 04_Right Image Bleed

This page places a vector illustration on the right and lets the image bleed from the right edge beyond the canvas. There is no border and no white space; the image simply spills out. On the left, the text floats over a crisp frost-white background, creating a strong visual tension between the large image and the small type.

---

# 05_Top Image, Three Columns

A full-width watercolor of mountains and lakes occupies more than half of the upper frame, while three columns of text are arranged at the bottom like image captions. The image flows downward into the text area, guiding the eye naturally from the expansive landscape to specific information. The layout creates a reading rhythm reminiscent of a header and footnotes.

---

# 06_Text Overlay on Bottom Image

A warm, hand-drawn sketch-style scene sits at the bottom of the page, with text floating above. The image is like a stage set, while the text takes the foreground role. A pastel palette gives the entire page the gentle quality characteristic of educational materials.

---

# 07_Z-Shaped Serpentine

When reading a page, the human eye naturally scans from upper left to lower right in a Z pattern. This page leverages that pattern—equally spaced isometric 3D technology visuals alternate with text, while the three sections flow along the Z shape. Two graphics with matching isometric perspectives create visual continuity.

---

# 08_3×3 Grid Mixed Layout

The entire page is divided into nine cells in a three-by-three grid. The center cell contains a blueprint-style circuit board image, while the surrounding eight cells each carry text descriptions, color swatches, or specifications. The image is centered and the information surrounds it—one of the densest forms of image-text juxtaposition.

---

# 09_Circular Radiance

A circularly cropped dashboard image floats at the exact center of the page, with six thin lines extending from its edges toward the four corners. Each line ends in a text annotation block. This is the logic of a product diagram—the image as the subject, the text as its footnotes, and centripetal tension fixing the viewer’s gaze on the center.

---

# 10_Tiled Background Pattern

A pixel-retro city nightscape fills the entire frame, with four information cards layered on top. The image is not the visual focus here, but an atmospheric carrier—it provides a warm background against which the text reads clearly. An overlay controls the opacity to ensure legibility.

---

# 11_Layered Paper-Cut Design

Five layers of paper-cut mountain scenery recede from foreground to background, with a text label embedded in each layer. The text does not float on top of the image; it is integrated into different layers of depth. This is a way to embed text within the internal structure of an image rather than simply overlaying it on the surface.

---

# 12_Vertical Magazine

The image occupies just 22 percent of the page width on the left—a narrow vertical strip, like a book spine. Large horizontal type spreads across the right, while the retro poster’s duotone treatment limits the entire page to two colors. The smaller the image, the greater the power of the type.

---

# 13_Diagonal Split

The page is divided by a diagonal line into two triangles—the upper left is a dreamlike forest in a fantasy animation style, while the lower right is a text area on a dark background. The boundary is neither vertical nor horizontal, but diagonal; this shift in angle creates a strong sense of movement and drama.

---

# 14_Faded Background Image with Large Text

The ink-wash landscape painting is reduced to low saturation and recedes into the page background, while oversized type is layered directly over it. The image becomes texture, and the text takes absolute center stage. This page reminds us that an image does not have to be presented in full; it can exist translucently as an atmospheric background.

---

# 15_Text Above, Image Below

The upper half features text, while the lower half presents a top-down view in a flat design style, separated by a gemstone-colored gradient line. Text and image each occupy half, creating an equal dialogue between them—with no hierarchy, only an up-and-down rhythm.

---

# 16_Center-Radial Callouts

A square image sits at the exact center of the page, with six lines radiating outward from its edges, each ending in a text annotation. This is the logic of a product manual: the image is the subject, the lines are the guides, and the text is the explanation. The minimalist Swiss-style design makes the entire composition resemble a precise engineering diagram.

---

# 17_Horizontal Bar Across

A screen-print-style horizontal image strip runs across the middle of the page, separating the two blocks of text above and below. Here, the image is not content but rhythm—it creates a forced pause, giving readers a moment to take a deep breath before continuing.

---

# 18_Polygonal Gap Filling

The image is cropped into a hexagon, while text freely fills the outer corners and the open space to the right. The non-rectangular container creates an organic sense of movement. Rather than sitting beside the image, the text finds gaps to fill—this is the freest form of image–text interaction.

---

# 19_Montage Collage

A collage of three similarly styled but distinct editorial photographs fills the entire frame, with oversized typography spanning all three images. The power of the montage lies in juxtaposition—a single image may not have much impact, but place three together and lay a large word over them, and the visual presence arrives.

---

# 20_Chalkboard Finale

Every combination is a language. There is no fixed formula between image and text, only infinite possibilities.
