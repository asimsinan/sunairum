# 03_The Magician of Color and Emotion

[Transition] So let's begin with the first element—color.

Color is a space’s first impression—it’s like an “emotional magician.” When you enter a room, the first thing you sense is not the brand of the furniture or the cost of the materials, but the color atmosphere of the space as a whole. Warm tones make you feel gently enveloped, while cool tones make the same area seem more expansive. [Pause] Different colors also trigger different psychological responses—gray-green inspires calm and focus, warm coffee tones create the comfort of being wrapped in sunlight, while small touches of accent color can give a space a distinctive personality.

Key points: ① Color creates the first impression ② Different spatial effects of warm and cool colors ③ The psychological mechanisms behind color responses
Duration: 1.5 minutes

---

# 04_Contrasting Warm and Cool Tones

[Transition] To understand the power of color more intuitively, let's look at two contrasting cases.

The space on the left uses light-colored walls, which expand the area reflecting natural light and make the entire room feel remarkably spacious and bright. On the right, more highly saturated colors create a compact, cozy feeling despite covering the same area. That is why professional designers always establish the overall color palette before getting started.

Key points: ① Light colors create a sense of spaciousness ② High saturation creates a sense of compactness ③ Color choices come before other design decisions

# 05_The Language of Material and Space

[Transition] Having covered color, let's move on to the second element—material.

When renovating, many people focus too much on visual effects and overlook the importance of materials. In fact, materials are the physical foundation of color coordination. When you enter a room, the floor beneath your feet, the walls before you, the ceiling overhead… each speaks to you in its own "material language." A matte wood floor radiates simplicity and warmth, while soft handwoven textiles convey ease and relaxation. [Pause] There is one more thing many people overlook—light is also a material in its own right. Different materials reveal entirely different textures under light. So creating a sense of refinement in a space does not necessarily require expensive materials; thoughtful lighting and the interplay of light and shadow can bring materials to life.

Key points: ① Material is the physical foundation of color ② Different materials convey different emotions ③ Light is also a material ④ Expensive materials are not required
Duration: 1.5 minutes

---

# 06_Texture: The Soul of Space

[Transition] The third element is texture—often considered the easiest to overlook, yet also the most soulful.

Texture is like the “fingerprint” of a space, giving each one a unique identity. We can understand it from two dimensions: tactile texture and visual texture. Tactile texture is something you can perceive directly—the soft warmth of a wool carpet beneath your bare feet, or the uneven surface of a rough ceramic vase beneath your fingertips. [Pause] Visual texture is subtler—when you look at wallpaper with a delicate pattern, your eyes can “feel” its texture even without touching it. Together, these two forms of texture breathe soul into a space.

Key points: ① Texture = the fingerprint of a space ② Direct perception of tactile texture ③ Imagination and sensation evoked by visual texture
Duration: 1.5 minutes

---

# 07_CMT System

[Transition] Now that we've understood the individual power of color, material, and texture, a key question arises: how can we organically bring the three together?

This leads to today's core methodology—the CMT system, the integrated application of Colour, Material, and Texture. It is not a simple addition of the three, but a systematic interweaving. [Pause] On a physical level, it conveys tactile differences between smooth and rough, transparent and opaque, glossy and matte. On a psychological level, it evokes rich emotional associations: handmade or industrial, modest or luxurious, comforting or stimulating. It's like a musician conducting a symphony orchestra, allowing color, material, and texture to resonate harmoniously in space. The two examples on the right demonstrate the practical effects of the CMT system.

Key points: ① CMT: a system of color + material + texture ② Dual perception on physical and psychological levels ③ Systematic weaving rather than simple layering
Duration: 2 minutes

# 08_The Evolution of Cream-Style Design

[Transition] With the CMT framework in place, let’s see how some of the most popular styles of 2026 apply it.

First is the cream aesthetic. It remains highly popular in 2026, but it is undergoing a profound "evolution." The cream aesthetic used to follow a formula—off-white walls paired with natural-wood furniture, all looking much the same. Now it is moving toward a more nuanced expression. [Pause] A truly sophisticated cream aesthetic needs to work on three levels at once: use cream and oatmeal tones as the color base; add tactile soft furnishings such as wool and linen; and retain the natural knots in raw wood and the marks of hand-thrown ceramic lamps... Layer upon layer, these details transform it into a healing space that can truly be touched and felt.

Key points: ① Moving cream-toned interiors from formulaic to nuanced ② Applying color, materials, and texture on three levels ③ A healing space = something tactile and perceptible
Duration: 1.5 minutes

# 09_Bohemian and Retro

[Transition] Next are two seemingly different styles with a shared essence.

The charm of Bohemian style lies in its freedom—bold colors, handwoven tapestries, ethnic-inspired cushions, tasseled curtains… The collision and fusion of different textures create a "layered sense of nonconformity." Free, yet never chaotic. [Pause] Nostalgic vintage style, meanwhile, touches the heart in another way—weathered wood, slightly rusted wrought iron, and wicker worn smooth by time… These materials, marked by use, are stories in themselves. They turn the home into a vessel for memories. What do the two styles have in common? A deep longing for authenticity and emotion.

Key points: ① Bohemian style = free-spirited clashes of texture ② Nostalgic vintage style = material carriers of a sense of time ③ Shared core: a longing for authenticity
Duration: 1.5 minutes

# 10_Sustainable Luxury

[Transition] The final style worth noting represents an entirely new set of values.

In 2026, "luxury" is being redefined. It is no longer a mere accumulation of expensive materials, but a form of "sustainable luxury" that is kind to the planet and stands the test of time. [Pause] Eco-friendly finishes, maximizing natural light, reclaimed wood, cork, jute fiber… True luxury is shifting from "conspicuous consumption" to "responsible aesthetics." In such spaces, we experience not only beauty, but also a philosophy of living in harmony with the world.

Key points: ① Redefining luxury ② Innovative applications of sustainable materials ③ The aesthetic shift from ostentation to responsibility
