[Transition] Plant dyeing was not invented by Li Ziqi's generation. Its history is far older than we imagine.

Historical records state: **"The art of dyeing dates back to the era of Xuanyuan. The Yellow Emperor established black caps and yellow robes, using the juice of plants to dye decorative patterns."**[Pause]

From the **Western Zhou dynasty**, when dedicated "dyers" were appointed, to the **Qin dynasty's** "Dyeing Office," then the **Tang and Song dynasties'** "Dyeing Institutes," and the **Ming and Qing dynasties'** "Indigo Office"—China's dyeing techniques gradually evolved from folk handicrafts into large-scale state-run production. Works such as *Essential Techniques for the Common People*, *The Exploitation of the Works of Nature*, *The Artificers' Record*, and *The Comprehensive Statutes of the Great Ming* systematically documented the techniques and processes of plant dyeing.

A dyeing river stretching back thousands of years still flows through Li Ziqi’s battle robe today.
