[Transition] Now let’s explore two more—**"Twilight Cloud Gray"** and **"Skywater Jade"**.

**Twilight Cloud Gray** is the dark purplish-red gray seen in the clouds at dusk. [Pause] Li Qingzhao wrote, “**The setting sun melts gold; twilight clouds form jade**,” while Liu Yong wrote, “Across a thousand miles, misty waves; dusk haze sinks deep beneath the vast Chu sky” — with a single gray, the ancients captured every emotion of twilight. The dyeing method is actually simple: sappanwood mordanted with blue vitriol and a small amount of green vitriol.

**Tianshui Bi** has a moving story. The consort of Li Yu, the last ruler of Southern Tang, once left incompletely dyed silk outside overnight. After absorbing the dew, the silk unexpectedly turned a vivid green. Li Yu named this accidental color **“Tianshui Bi”** — a single night of morning dew gave rise to a color name.
