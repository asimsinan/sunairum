[Transition] If what I just discussed were “colors of nature,” the next two are more like “poetic colors”—they are not merely colors, but also carry literary imagery.

**"Silver red"** is a pale crimson, like the first tint of dawn or peach petals touched with frost. It appears repeatedly in *Dream of the Red Chamber*—that soft gauze came in only four colors: rain-cleared sky blue, autumn fragrance, pine green, and **silver red**, also called "rosy-shadow gauze." [Pause] The wash of silver-red rosy-shadow gauze before Daiyu's window is just like the rouge mark stained by the tears of the Crimson Pearl Fairy Grass.

**"Tuo red"** is the flush that appears on the face after drinking. Li Bai wrote: "**A beauty, on the verge of drunkenness, her cheeks glow red**." Tuo red evokes the scene of "the imperial concubine drunk on wine"—how utterly alluring.
