# 01_cover

In 2026, new works by a group of Pritzker Prize masters will be unveiled almost in unison, making the year a true season of new works by Pritzker laureates. Unlike the fleeting appeal of influencer architecture, these projects reflect craftsmanship refined over time and a profound integration of architecture, nature, culture, and the spirit of the age. Today, let’s step into eight masterworks that will open or be completed in 2026 and explore the ideas and convictions of the world’s leading architects.

---

# 02_overview

Before we officially begin, let’s take in the big picture. Eight masters span four continents—Asia, Europe, Africa, and the Americas—from Tadao Ando in Suzhou, China, to Kéré in Dakar, Senegal; from the only newly built venue for the Milan Winter Olympics to Los Angeles’s art ark, completed after twenty years. Eight identities, eight propositions—but they have all entrusted their work to 2026. Next, we’ll explore them one by one.

---

# 03_ando

First stop: Suzhou. In January 2026, the H+ Art Museum, designed by Tadao Ando, officially opened on the banks of the Shangtang River in Gusu District. Ando said that, as an architect from outside the region, his greatest concern was how to draw the essence of classical gardens from Suzhou’s cultural memory. Layered gardens and circular circulation are the museum’s core concepts—creating depth within a limited space and conveying the passage of time through water and light. From the cool severity of concrete to the tenderness of light, and from geometric order to the freedom of gardens, Ando has completed a profound dialogue between Eastern aesthetics and Western modernism on the banks of the Shangtang River in the ancient city.

---

# 04_chipperfield

Second stop: Milan. This is the only new permanent venue built for the 2026 Milan–Cortina Winter Olympics—the San Giulia Ice Hockey Arena, designed by David Chipperfield. The elliptical venue, which seats 16,000 spectators, echoes the ancient Roman amphitheaters of a city with 2,000 years of history. Three metal rings, a glass belt, and LED light strips transform the façade into a dynamic screen at the scale of the city. Chipperfield never relies on extravagant forms to attract attention; instead, with utmost restraint and precision, he explores architecture’s relationship with history and urban public life.

---

# 05_zaha

Third stop: the Tamsui River estuary in Taipei. Opened to traffic on May 12, 2026, the Tamsui River Bridge is one of the final projects completed by the firm Zaha Hadid was part of during her lifetime. Stretching 920 meters, it is the longest-span single-tower asymmetric cable-stayed bridge of its kind in the world. Most moving of all, after numerous rounds of design challenges, the project ultimately realized Zaha’s nearly idealized vision with close to 100% fidelity. Though the master is gone, her architectural spirit of breaking boundaries and refusing limits continues to thrive in this bridge.

---

# 06_foster

Fourth stop: Shanghai. In April 2026, Norman Foster personally unveiled the Jia Art Space, designed by him and his team, on the banks of the Suzhou Creek. Rather than a traditional white box, the gallery resembles a living organism slowly growing within the city. Its façade mimics the growth of flowers taking root in the ground and reaching toward the sky, transforming structural beauty from the inside out into unfolding petals. Beneath the high-tech aesthetic shell, Foster is really asking how the classical humanist spirit can be reborn through modern materials.

---

# 07_oma_sanaa

Fifth stop: New York. This is a rare collaboration between two powerhouses. OMA, led by Rem Koolhaas, completed the new wing, while SANAA, founded by Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa, completed the main building back in 2010. With an $82 million investment and more than 5,500 square meters of additional space, it officially opened to the public on March 21, 2026. OMA’s pioneering deconstructivist thinking collides with SANAA’s light, transparent aesthetic, allowing this century-old museum to preserve its artistic core while gaining a design language suited to contemporary forms of expression. It is the perfect embodiment of iterative renewal and inclusivity.

---

# 08_zumthor

Sixth stop: Los Angeles. This building took 20 years to complete—the LACMA David Geffen Galleries, designed by Peter Zumthor, officially opened on May 4, 2026. With a $724 million investment and more than 30,000 square meters of floor area, seven minimalist concrete pavilions are arranged in a staggered formation, floating above busy Wilshire Boulevard. With 20 years of patience, Zumthor resisted the impatience of the age and ultimately realized his famous idea: architecture is a poetic container for place. This is not a landmark that flaunts itself, but a pure home where artworks can converse with the Los Angeles sky.

---

# 09_gehry

Seventh stop: Abu Dhabi. This is the final masterpiece left to the world by architectural maverick Frank Gehry. Gehry passed away in December 2025, while the Guggenheim Abu Dhabi, to which he devoted nearly two decades, is expected to open in 2026. Irregular curves, fluid folds, and highly dramatic geometric forms define the building, along with a shading system tailored to the desert’s extreme climate. Its metal façade shifts in texture as the sunlight moves. As the deconstructivist master’s swan song, this building offers Gehry’s final interpretation of his belief that architecture has no boundaries and creativity no limits.

---

# 10_kere

Eighth stop: Dakar, Senegal. Kéré Architecture’s Goethe-Institut opened in April 2026. A baobab tree symbolizing life stands at the heart of the central courtyard, while locally compressed earth bricks form the primary structure, complemented by a natural ventilation system that eliminates the need for air conditioning. This is Kéré’s consistent design philosophy: work with nature rather than against it. As architecture around the world grows increasingly homogenized, Kéré once again proves that great architecture is never one-size-fits-all luxury, but a humane commitment rooted in the land and capable of warming the heart.

---

# 11_epilogue

After visiting these eight buildings, we have also completed our journey through the 2026 season of new works by Pritzker laureates. Some spent twenty years refining a single venue, using extraordinary patience to resist the impatience of the times; some remained committed to local culture, allowing architecture to take root in the land and warm the heart; others broke boundaries and overturned conventions, using creativity to expand architecture’s infinite possibilities. This feast has taught us that all timeless classics stem from day after day of perseverance, from the deepest reverence and reflection on space, nature, and humanity.
